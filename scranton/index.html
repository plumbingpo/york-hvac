When Your System Fails in Scranton

If your system quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep our schedule flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat as top priority.

These are the problems that come up most when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has gone blank. Nearly all of these trace back to parts already on our trucks: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and standard fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

Two situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can flood the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or produces a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. That small step can prevent a far bigger repair.

How Scranton Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and also shows rising bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement in peak season.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a miserable night.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Scranton

Most local HVAC failures give warning signs before they happen. A breaker that trips every few days, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, energy bills creeping up with nothing else changed. These are the early symptoms of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Left alone, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
